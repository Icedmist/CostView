CostView — MVP Flow & Layout PRD
How the product delivers on “Fast, Efficient, Secure”

1. Starting point
The founder story defines the promise in three words: Fast. Efficient. Secure.
Secure is already solved. The PWA + cloud architecture means project data is never tied
to one device, one laptop, one phone. That was the whole point of the story — that’s done at
the infrastructure level, not the workflow level.
This document is about the other two: Fast and Efficient. Not as slogans, but as specific
things the MVP does differently from the traditional method (paper files, one laptop,
WhatsApp, manual spreadsheet harmonizing) — grounded in what is already built in the
current prototype.
Working definitions for this document:
Fast = less typing, less re-entry, less waiting for information that already exists
somewhere else in the system.
Efficient = the right person sees the right thing at the right time, without someone
having to chase, compile, or manually route it.

2. The core mechanic: one flow, not six silos
The traditional method fails because each piece of information lives in its own silo — a
requisition on paper, a PO in someone’s email, a delivery note in a site file, an invoice on an
accountant’s desk — and someone has to manually walk between all of them to reconcile
the numbers.
CostView’s MVP is built around Operations, a single connected flow of seven modules that
all point back to the same budget and the same audit trail:
Budget → Procurement → Materials → Labour → Progress → Subcontractors →
Variations

The Fast/Efficient gain doesn’t come from any one module in isolation. It comes from the
fact that a number entered once in one module automatically shows up, correctly,
everywhere downstream — instead of being retyped by three different people into three
different documents.

3. The flow, end to end
1. Budget (Overview, BOQ, Forecast, Cost Control, Revisions, Final Account) The BOQ is the
anchor. Every cost line downstream — a purchase order, a labour cost, a variation —
references a BOQ item, so cost control and forecast update themselves instead of being
rebuilt from scratch every reporting cycle. → Fast: no manual spreadsheet harmonizing to
see where the project stands. → Efficient: one true version of the budget everyone works
against, instead of five people’s personal copies.
2. Procurement (Requisitions → Supplier Enquiries → Purchase Orders → Deliveries →
Invoices → Payments → Supplier Performance) This is the clearest example of the threedocument chain: a Requisition becomes a PO, the PO is matched against the GRN
(delivery), and the Invoice/Payment is matched against both. Nobody re-types quantities
or prices at each stage — they carry forward. → Fast: a requisition-to-payment cycle that
took phone calls and paper trails now moves through pre-filled, linked documents. →
Efficient: nothing gets paid that wasn’t ordered, and nothing gets ordered that wasn’t
delivered — the three-way match happens automatically, not by someone manually crosschecking three files.
3. Materials (Inventory, GRN, Issues, Returns, Transfers, Stock Count) Site-level stock is
tracked against the same GRNs raised in Procurement — a delivery isn’t recorded twice. →
Fast: store staff aren’t re-writing what procurement already recorded. → Efficient: real-time
inventory means no over-ordering or site stock-outs discovered too late.
4. Labour (Attendance, Payroll, Productivity, Timesheets) Attendance feeds payroll and
productivity automatically instead of a foreman’s notebook being manually transcribed at
month-end. → Fast: payroll doesn’t wait on someone re-keying a paper attendance register.
→ Efficient: productivity numbers are a by-product of daily attendance, not a separate
report someone has to build.
5. Progress (Diary, Photos, Inspections, Snags, Safety, Quality) This is the module that
saved the founder story — the site diary that lived on the cloud, not the laptop. Photos,
inspections, and snags are logged where they happen and are immediately visible to anyone
with access, not bundled into a report days later. → Fast: the progress report is a live view,
not something compiled overnight before a meeting. → Efficient: an inspection finding
routes straight into the snag list — it doesn’t need a second person to translate a paper note

into a task.
6. Subcontractors (Contracts, Claims, Certificates, Payments, Performance) A
subcontractor’s claim references their contract terms directly, and payment references the
certificate — the same linked-document logic as Procurement, applied to subcontract
accounts. → Fast: no manually cross-checking a claim against a contract stored somewhere
else. → Efficient: certified-but-unpaid and claimed-but-uncertified amounts are visible at a
glance.
7. Variations (Register, Approval Workflow, Cost Impact, Instructions) A variation’s cost
impact is calculated against the live budget automatically, and the threshold-based approval
workflow routes it to the right approver without anyone having to know who to send it to. →
Fast: cost impact is instant, not a re-calculation exercise. → Efficient: approval routing is
automatic — the system knows who signs off based on value, not a phone call to find out.

4. What makes the whole thing fast (cross-cutting)
Document linking, not document re-entry — Requisition → PO → GRN → Invoice →
Payment is one continuous record, not five separate files someone has to reconcile by
hand.
Live dashboards instead of compiled reports — budget position, procurement funnel,
top suppliers, and attention-needed items are always current, not assembled the night
before a meeting.
One place to look — no switching between paper diary, WhatsApp, Excel, and email to
reconstruct what happened on site this week.

5. What makes the whole thing efficient (cross-cutting)
Threshold-based approvals — variations and payments route automatically to the
correct approver based on value, removing the “who do I send this to” step entirely.
Full audit trail — every entry is attributable and timestamped, so there’s no versionmerging chaos from five people’s spreadsheet copies.
Role-based views — each of the six user roles sees only what’s relevant to their work,
so people aren’t wading through modules that aren’t theirs to act on.

7. Scope note
This PRD covers the Operations flow only — the seven modules above are the core MVP
that every user role touches. The Commercial (Contractor/Developer toggle) modules
already scaffolded in the prototype (tendering, contracts, feasibility, project finance, etc.) sit
on top of this core and are a later layer, not part of the MVP’s fast/efficient case — they
extend what the platform does, not how the day-to-day flow works.
