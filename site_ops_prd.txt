CostView — Site Operations PRD
What this covers: Budget, Procurement, Materials, Labour, Progress, Subcontractors,
Variations, Reports, Admin, Help. Not covered: Commercial mode (Contractor/Developer
toggle).
Why this document exists: to tell developers, in plain terms, what each screen shows,
where every number comes from, what the system should do on its own, and what a person
still needs to do by hand — including everything the current prototype shows but doesn’t
actually let you do yet.
The one rule behind everything below: let the system calculate whatever it honestly can.
A person only gets involved to (1) type in something the system has no way of knowing — a
name, a decision, a brand-new item — or (2) approve/reject something the system has
already flagged for them. Nobody should be typing numbers the system could work out
itself, and nothing should sit stuck waiting on a person unless it’s genuinely an exception.

1. Budget
1.1 Overview
Just a dashboard. Every number on it is pulled from the tabs below. Nobody types anything
here.

1.2 BOQ — this is where real data enters the system
Someone uploads their BOQ, material list, labour list, or rebar schedule in whatever format
they already have — Excel, Word, even a scanned PDF. The AI reads it and sorts it into the
right columns (Item, Description, Qty, Unit, Rate, Amount). Before anything gets saved, the
person reviews what the AI pulled out, fixes anything it got wrong, and confirms — only then
does it go live.
Every item gets a unique code so it can be linked to its material, labour, and rebar costs
elsewhere in the system. Actual cost, budget vs. actual variance, and status (On Budget /
Over / Under) all calculate themselves from there.
Two things get added once, per item, at the start: who’s expected to finish it and by
when, and who’s responsible for it.

Still missing: a way to manually add a BOQ item (for anything the import missed), a way to
edit or delete a row, and the confirm-before-saving step described above.

1.3 Forecast — fully automatic, nothing to type
Works out where the project’s costs are heading based on what’s actually happened so far,
plus what’s left to do at the rate it’s currently costing. Shows expected total cost, how far
that is from budget, how confident that number is, and when the project is likely to finish —
compared against the planned finish date, so you can see if you’re ahead or behind. Nothing
here needs manual entry, and nothing is missing.

1.4 Cost Control — fully automatic, nothing to type
Shows how efficiently money is being spent against how much work is actually done, flags
anything trending over budget, and shows who’s responsible for each item.
Still missing: a way to mark a flagged risk as “seen/handled” once someone’s dealt with it.

1.5 Revisions — a person creates it, the system applies it
A Revision is a real, approved change to the budget — always tied to something that actually
happened: an approved Variation, a rate that was wrong, or a client-instructed change.
Still missing, entirely: a button to add a new Revision (what changed, why, the ₦ amount,
which BOQ item it affects); an automatic pre-filled entry whenever a Variation gets
approved, so nobody has to retype it; and an Approve/Reject action on each one. Only an
approved Revision should actually change the budget — and once it does, that change
should flow through to Forecast, Cost Control, and Final Account on its own.

1.6 Final Account — fully automatic, and reconciliation never gets stuck
Compares the original budget against what was actually spent, item by item. Every item
reconciles automatically — a variance never holds that up. If there’s a gap, it’s shown right
next to the “Reconciled” tag (e.g. “Reconciled — Variance: +₦410,000”), and it shows up in
management reports too, not hidden anywhere.
Still missing: a simple way for the QS to leave a note on a flagged variance (wastage, theft,
a negotiated rate — whatever the reason is) for the record. This is just a note for the audit
trail — it never changes the Reconciled status.

2. Procurement
The chain here is: Requisition → Supplier Enquiry → Purchase Order → Delivery →

Invoice → Payment, plus a Supplier Performance score built from all of it. Each document
remembers which document it came from, so you can always trace the whole trail back.

2.1 Overview
Dashboard only, pulled from the tabs below.

2.2 Requisitions
Gets created two ways: automatically (when material stock runs low) or manually (a Site
Engineer needs something for site).
Still missing: a “New Requisition” form for the manual path, and an Approve/Reject action.

2.3 Supplier Enquiries
Sent out to suppliers for pricing, responses compared side by side, then converted straight
into a Purchase Order.
Still missing: a way to actually create a new enquiry, and every action button on this screen
(Add Supplier, Compare Quotes, Select Supplier, Convert to PO, etc.) needs to actually work
— right now they just sit there.

2.4 Purchase Orders
Created from a converted Enquiry (or directly).
Still missing: a proper approval step before it goes to the supplier — right now it jumps
straight from “created” to “supplier accepted” with nobody signing off in between. Also
needs a way to pick how payment works for this order — pay first, pay after delivery, or
partial upfront — since that decides whether an invoice can be raised before delivery
happens.

2.5 Deliveries
Same record as the GRN in Materials — it shouldn’t be entered twice in two places.
Delivered quantity is checked against the order automatically, and any shortfall gets
flagged.
Still missing: a real “Verify Delivery” / “Resolve Discrepancy” action — right now it’s just a
static screen.

2.6 Invoices
Matched against the Purchase Order (and the delivery, once there is one). The system
checks all three match up and flags anything that doesn’t.

Still missing: a way to actually log a new invoice, and working buttons for flagging a
discrepancy, requesting a credit note, and downloading.

2.7 Payments
Tracks back through Invoice → Order → Delivery. Needs to support paying in advance when
that’s the agreed terms.
Still missing: “Process Payment” and “Hold Payment” need to actually do something —
record how it was paid, when, and update the invoice status.

2.8 Supplier Performance
On-time delivery and pricing can be worked out automatically from order/delivery/invoice
history.
Still missing: quality is a judgment call, not something the system can work out on its own
— needs a simple “Rate This Delivery” action for whoever receives the goods, which then
feeds the supplier’s score over time.

3. Materials
Handled by the Site Engineer, acting as storekeeper for this section (same person, no
separate role needed).

3.1 Inventory
Shows stock levels and status (Healthy / Low / Out) per material. Read-only overview, click
into any item for detail. Nothing missing here.

3.2 GRN (delivery record)
Created automatically when a Purchase Order is approved — nobody types this in by hand.
The person’s job is just to confirm the count matches, or flag it if it doesn’t. Nothing
missing.

3.3 Consumption (material sent to site)
Flags automatically if more is used than the work should have needed.
Still missing: a way to actually record a new consumption entry — right now you can only
confirm or reverse ones that already exist.

3.4 Returns
Always linked back to the delivery it came from. Person decides: restock, dispose, or reject.
Nothing missing — this correctly starts from an existing record, not a blank form.

3.5 Transfers
Moving stock between sites/stores.
Still missing: a “Create Transfer” button — right now there’s no way to start one.

3.6 Stock Count
Pulls the list of what to count automatically from Inventory. Person counts, system compares
against what it expects, differences get reviewed. Nothing missing.

4. Labour
Two tabs only.

4.1 Payroll
Still missing: a way to add a worker (name, trade, pay period, daily rate) — this is the actual
starting point for the whole module and it doesn’t exist yet.

4.2 Timesheets
Pulls from whoever’s already in Payroll — nothing separately typed here. Tracks hours and
overtime, with an approval trail. Nothing missing.

5. Progress
5.1 Diary
Day-by-day site journal. Right now you can only add an entry inside a day that’s already
been started.
Still missing: a way to start a brand-new day/entry from scratch.

5.2 Photos
A gallery of everything attached in Diary.

Still missing: a direct way to upload a photo — right now the only way in is through a Diary
entry.

5.3 Inspections
Still missing: a way to add a new inspection — right now you can only view existing ones.

5.4 Snags (defects to fix)
Correctly created only from an inspection (“Raise Snag”) — not something typed from
scratch. Nothing missing.

5.5 Safety
Right now this is just a static list — no way to add anything, no way to click in for detail.
Still missing: a way to log a new safety observation or incident. As it stands, this tab can’t
actually be used.

5.6 Quality
Non-conformance reports get raised automatically from a failed inspection — correctly not
something typed from scratch. Nothing missing.

6. Subcontractors
6.1 Contracts
This should be where a subcontractor first gets added to the project.
Still missing: a way to add a subcontractor — their name, trade, what they’re doing on site,
contract value, retention held. Clicking into one should show their real financial summary,
not a placeholder.

6.2 Claims
A claim is what a subcontractor submits for work done.
Still missing: a way to log a new claim — subcontractor, period, amount, what it’s for.

6.3 Certificates
This is the output once a claim is certified — view and download only, which is correct. It
just needs to actually pull from a real certified claim instead of showing static examples.

6.4 Payments
The ledger here — what’s been paid, what’s held as retention — should build itself from
Claims and Certificates, not be typed separately.

6.5 Performance
Right now it just shows a fixed score with nothing behind it.
Still missing: a way to actually grade a subcontractor across the four things that matter —
quality of work, keeping to schedule, safety, responsiveness — so the score means
something and updates over time, instead of being a fixed number.

7. Variations
Order these tabs: Instructions first, then Register, then Approval Workflow, then Cost
Impact — since an instruction is usually what triggers a variation in the first place.

7.1 Instructions
Still missing: a way to log a new instruction (who issued it, when, what it says), which can
then be linked to a variation once one is raised from it.

7.2 Register
The master list of variations.
Still missing: a way to raise a new variation — who raised it, what it’s for, which instruction
(if any) it’s linked to, which BOQ item it affects.

7.3 Approval Workflow
Draft → QS values it → PM reviews it → Approved. (No separate client-approval stage — PM
approval is the final internal step.)
Still missing: real buttons to move a variation through each stage — right now the board
just shows numbers with nothing to click.

7.4 Cost Impact
Fully automatic — once QS has valued a variation, the totals (additions, omissions, net
impact) and the chart calculate themselves. Nothing typed here.
Once approved: the system should automatically create a pre-filled Revision in Budget, so

nobody has to retype it there.

8. Reports
One of the most important sections — this is where everything gets reconciled and
reviewed.
Every report needs a project picker at the top — look at one project, or all of them
combined.
What’s needed here, kept simple: basic financial summaries a person can pull up
whenever they want to check how the numbers are doing — budget vs. actual, what’s been
spent, what’s committed, what’s left, cash position. Whatever cost question someone has,
there should be a report that answers it, with real figures and tables, not vague summaries.
A site progress report, built from the Diary/Inspections/Quality data, with actual numbers in
it — not just a narrative.
Nothing to type here — Reports is entirely a read/export layer on top of everything already
entered elsewhere. The only thing missing is that it needs to actually show real detail and let
you pick which project(s) to look at — right now it’s too thin.

9. Admin
Permissions — someone with admin access decides who can see and do what. This
one genuinely has to stay manual — access is a judgment call, not something the system
can decide on its own.
Audit Log — right now it’s just a scrolling feed.
Still missing: turn it into a proper searchable table — who did what, in which module, when,
and what changed — so if someone needs to trace a specific event on a specific day, they
can actually search for it instead of scrolling.

10. Help
A plain-language walkthrough of the whole platform, plus a phone number/email for anyone
who’s stuck and needs a person. Nothing calculated here — it’s just a guide.

Two Things Still To Confirm
1. Budget status threshold — how far off budget before an item is marked “Over” or
“Under” instead of “On Budget”? Suggesting ±5% as a starting point, but this is your
call.
2. Reports detail — this document lists the report types needed (budget vs. actual, cash
position, what’s spent/committed/left). The exact layout and fields inside each one is
worth a dedicated follow-up once you’ve had a chance to look at the report types you
actually use day to day.
